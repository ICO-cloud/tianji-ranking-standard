## Purpose

This annex provides a detailed mapping between the IREG Berlin Principles on Ranking of Higher Education Institutions (adopted 20 May 2006, Berlin) and the corresponding provisions of ICO Std 2002. It demonstrates the degree of alignment between the two frameworks and identifies areas where ICO Std 2002 extends beyond the requirements of the Berlin Principles.

## A.1 Overview of the Berlin Principles

### A.1.1 History and Context

The Berlin Principles on Ranking of Higher Education Institutions were adopted on 20 May 2006 at the second meeting of the International Ranking Expert Group (IREG), founded in 2004 by the UNESCO European Centre for Higher Education (UNESCO-CEPES) in Bucharest and the Institute for Higher Education Policy (IHEP) in Washington, DC. The principles consist of 16 items organised into four sections:

| --------- | ------- | -----------

| B | Design and Weighting of Indicators | R6–R9 
| C | Collection and Processing of Data | R10–R14 
| D | Presentation of Ranking Results | R15–R16 

> **Note**: The Berlin Principles were originally designed for higher education rankings. ICO Std 2002 applies to a broader range of ranking domains (cultural heritage, consumer products, brands, cities, etc.). The mapping below accounts for this domain expansion by identifying where ICO Std 2002 generalises or extends the Berlin Principles beyond their original higher education context.

## A.4 ICO Extensions Beyond Berlin Principles

The following features of ICO Std 2002 have no direct counterpart in the Berlin Principles and represent extensions beyond the scope of the original IREG framework:

ICO Std 2002，IREG：

### A.4.1 Four-Level Transparency System

The Berlin Principles require transparency in general terms (R6). ICO Std 2002 establishes a graduated four-level transparency classification (§9.2) that provides:

（R6）。ICO Std 2002（§9.2），：

- **Level 1: Summary Transparency** — minimum disclosure set

- **Level 2: Methodology Transparency** — full methodology documentation

- **Level 3: Data Transparency** — full data access for verification

- **Level 4: Full Reproducibility** — complete data and code for independent replication

This graduated system enables stakeholders to assess the transparency of a ranking publication against a defined standard and provides a pathway for ranking entities to progressively improve their transparency.

### A.4.2 DPP-CQ Integration Framework

The Data Processing Pipeline — Continuous Quality (DPP-CQ) integration framework (§10.4) provides a systematic mechanism for continuous quality improvement of ranking processes. This has no counterpart in the Berlin Principles, which address quality assurance only in general terms (R13).

—（DPP-CQ）（§10.4）。，（R13）。

The DPP-CQ framework includes:
DPP-CQ：

a) automated data quality monitoring throughout the ranking lifecycle;

b) trigger-based quality alerts for data anomalies;

c) feedback loops from ranking results to methodological improvement;

d) version control and change tracking for all methodological decisions.

### A.4.3 Domain Neutrality and Multi-Domain Architecture

The Berlin Principles were designed specifically for higher education rankings. ICO Std 2002 generalises the ranking methodology framework to apply across all domains through a core-layer + extension-module architecture. This domain-neutral design (§4.2.1) ensures that the standard's requirements are applicable to any ranking context while allowing domain-specific adaptations through extension modules (SE-01 through SE-09).

。ICO Std 2002+，。（§4.2.1），（SE-01SE-09）。

### A.4.4 Cultural Sensitivity Requirements

While the Berlin Principles acknowledge the importance of cultural context (R5), ICO Std 2002 extends this by incorporating explicit cultural sensitivity requirements in the SE-05 (Cultural Heritage) extension module and by requiring that all ranking systems consider the cultural implications of their indicator design and data collection methods. This is particularly relevant for the Tianji Ranking's intangible cultural heritage track.

### A.4.5 Anti-Fraud and Anti-Manipulation Mechanisms

The Berlin Principles do not address the risk of deliberate manipulation of ranking data by ranked entities. ICO Std 2002 includes explicit anti-fraud provisions (§7.5) that address:

。ICO Std 2002（§7.5），：

a) detection and prevention of data manipulation (e.g., fabricated survey responses, inflated metrics);

### A.4.6 UN DCP Alignment

ICO Std 2002 aligns with the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP) and the UNESCO Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage (2003), providing a framework for ranking systems that respect the rights and dignity of communities and cultural practitioners. ## A.5 Comparative Analysis

### A.5.1 Overall Assessment

ICO Std 2002 achieves full coverage of all 16 Berlin Principles, with 6 of 16 principles exceeded by ICO Std 2002's more detailed or broader provisions. No Berlin Principle is partially covered or uncovered. The areas of extension are primarily in:

ICO Std 200216，6ICO Std 2002。。：

a) **Transparency granularity** — the four-level transparency system provides a graduated framework not present in the Berlin Principles;

b) **Data quality rigour** — quantitative data quality metrics and the DPP-CQ framework provide systematic quality assurance beyond the Berlin Principles' general requirements;
——DPP-CQ；

c) **Anti-fraud provisions** — explicit mechanisms for preventing and detecting data manipulation;

d) **Domain scope** — generalisation from higher education to all ranking domains;

e) **Cultural sensitivity** — explicit requirements for cultural sensitivity in heritage and community contexts;

f) **User empowerment** — the user-adjustable weights framework provides a mechanism for consumer choice beyond the Berlin Principles' general recommendation.

### A.5.2 Implications for IREG Ranking Audit

An ICO Std 2002-compliant ranking system that covers the higher education domain would satisfy all IREG Ranking Audit criteria derived from the Berlin Principles. The additional provisions of ICO Std 2002 (transparency levels, data quality metrics, anti-fraud mechanisms) would provide additional evidence of quality that could strengthen the ranking's audit assessment.
