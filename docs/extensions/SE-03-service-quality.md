# SE-03 : Service Quality

**ICO Std 2002 — Extension SE-03**

---

This extension module specifies the requirements and methodologies for ranking and evaluating service quality. Service quality ranking presents distinctive challenges compared to product evaluation: services are inherently intangible and heterogeneous; production and consumption are often simultaneous; and the customer's experience is shaped by interactions across multiple service encounters and touchpoints. This module extends the core-layer methodology (Chapters 4 through 10) by adapting the SERVQUAL framework for ranking purposes, defining service-specific indicators, establishing customer satisfaction index (CSI) calculation methods, and providing protocols for service blueprinting and touchpoint analysis. It is applicable to rankings of service providers across all sectors, including hospitality, healthcare, financial services, education, and public services.

---

## SE-03.1 Scope and Applicable Objects

### SE-03.1.1 Scope

This module applies to the design, execution, and publication of rankings that evaluate the quality of services provided by organisations. It covers:

a) commercial services, including hospitality, financial services, retail, transportation, telecommunications, and professional services;

b) public services, including healthcare, education, government administrative services, and social welfare services;

c) digital services, including online platforms, software-as-a-service, and digital content services.

### SE-03.1.2 Applicable Objects

This module shall apply to rankings of:

a) individual service providers (e.g., hospitals, hotels, universities);

b) service provider networks or chains (e.g., hotel chains, retail networks);

c) service categories within a geographic or sectoral scope.

### SE-03.1.3 Exclusions

This module does not apply to:

a) the evaluation of physical products (see SE-01);

b) regulatory compliance audits of service providers;

c) individual employee performance evaluation.

## SE-03.3 Indicator System

### SE-03.3.1 Indicator Hierarchy

The service quality indicator system shall be organised into the following three-level hierarchy based on an extended SERVQUAL model:

a) **Level 1 — Dimensions**: Tangibles, Reliability, Responsiveness, Assurance, Empathy;

b) **Level 2 — Sub-dimensions**: Decompositions of each dimension as specified in SE-03.3.2 through SE-03.3.6;

c) **Level 3 — Specific measures**: Operationalised variables for which data is collected.

### SE-03.3.2 Tangibles

Tangibles assess the physical evidence of the service, including facilities, equipment, personnel appearance, and communication materials.

a) **Physical Facilities**: The quality and condition of the service environment, including:

1) cleanliness and maintenance of premises;

2) architectural design and spatial layout appropriate to the service type;

3) accessibility features for persons with disabilities.

b) **Equipment and Technology**: The modernity, functionality, and reliability of equipment and technology used in service delivery, including:

1) the currency and adequacy of technological infrastructure;

2) the functionality of self-service and digital touchpoints;

3) the reliability of equipment during peak service demand.

c) **Professional Appearance**: The appearance, grooming, and professionalism of service personnel as presented to customers, including:

1) uniform or dress code compliance;

2) identification badges and credentials;

3) body language and demeanour consistent with professional service standards.

d) **Communication Materials**: The quality, clarity, and professionalism of physical and digital communication materials, including signage, brochures, websites, and mobile applications.

### SE-03.3.3 Reliability

Reliability assesses the ability to deliver the promised service dependably and accurately.

a) **Service Consistency**: The degree to which the service is delivered consistently across different times, locations, and service encounters. The ranking entity shall measure:

1) the variance in service outcomes across repeated transactions;

2) the standardisation of service processes;

3) the degree of compliance with published service standards.

b) **Promise Fulfilment**: The degree to which the service provider fulfils its explicit and implicit service promises, including:

1) adherence to published schedules and timelines;

2) accuracy of information provided to customers;

3) delivery of promised service features and benefits.

c) **Error Rate**: The frequency and severity of service errors, including:

1) the rate of service failures per 1,000 transactions;

2) the rate of billing or documentation errors;

3) the severity classification of errors (minor, moderate, critical).

d) **Service Recovery**: The effectiveness of the service provider's response when service failures occur, including:

1) the speed of failure acknowledgment and response;

2) the resolution rate of complaints and service recovery cases;

3) customer satisfaction with service recovery outcomes.

### SE-03.3.4 Responsiveness

Responsiveness assesses the willingness to help customers and provide prompt service.

a) **Response Time**: The elapsed time from customer request to initial service provider response. The ranking entity shall measure:

1) average first-response time by channel (in-person, telephone, email, chat);

2) the percentage of responses within published service level targets;

3) response time consistency across peak and off-peak periods.

b) **Wait Time**: The elapsed time that customers spend waiting for service, including:

1) physical queue waiting time;

2) appointment delay time;

3) digital service loading and processing time.

c) **Proactive Communication**: The degree to which the service provider proactively communicates with customers about service status, changes, or issues, including:

1) advance notification of service changes or disruptions;

2) progress updates during service delivery;

3) follow-up communication after service completion.

d) **Channel Accessibility**: The availability and accessibility of service channels, including:

1) the range of service channels offered (physical, telephone, digital);

2) operating hours and service availability;

3) multilingual service capability.

### SE-03.3.5 Assurance

Assurance assesses the knowledge, courtesy, and ability of service personnel to inspire trust and confidence.

a) **Professional Competence**: The depth and breadth of knowledge and skill demonstrated by service personnel, including:

1) the proportion of service personnel with relevant professional qualifications or certifications;

2) the accuracy and completeness of information provided by service personnel;

3) the ability to handle complex or non-standard service requests.

b) **Trustworthiness**: The degree to which customers perceive the service provider as honest, ethical, and trustworthy, including:

1) the transparency of service terms, pricing, and conditions;

2) the protection of customer data and privacy;

3) the absence of misleading claims or pressure tactics.

c) **Safety and Security**: The degree to which the service environment and processes ensure customer safety and security, including:

1) physical safety measures and protocols;

2) cybersecurity and data protection measures;

3) insurance and liability coverage.

d) **Credentialing and Accreditation**: The extent to which the service provider holds relevant industry certifications and accreditations, including:

1) quality management certifications (e.g., ISO 9001);

2) industry-specific certifications and licences;

3) professional body memberships and accreditations.

### SE-03.3.6 Empathy

Empathy assesses the caring, individualised attention the service provider gives to customers.

a) **Personalisation**: The degree to which the service is tailored to individual customer needs and preferences, including:

1) the use of customer data to personalise service interactions;

2) the flexibility of service processes to accommodate individual circumstances;

3) the recognition of returning or loyal customers.

b) **Customer Understanding**: The degree to which service personnel demonstrate understanding of customer needs and concerns, including:

1) active listening and needs assessment;

2) the accuracy of needs identification;

3) the appropriateness of recommended solutions.

c) **Accessibility for Vulnerable Groups**: The degree to which the service is accessible and accommodating to vulnerable or special-needs populations, including:

1) accessibility for persons with disabilities;

2) language and cultural accommodations;

3) age-appropriate service adaptations.

d) **Emotional Support**: The degree to which service personnel provide emotional support and reassurance, particularly in stressful or sensitive service contexts (e.g., healthcare, legal services), including:

1) the demonstration of empathy and compassion;

2) the management of customer anxiety or distress;

3) the provision of clear and reassuring communication.

## SE-03.4 Service Blueprint and Touchpoint Analysis

### SE-03.4.1 Service Blueprint Requirements

a) The ranking entity shall develop or reference a service blueprint for each service type being ranked. The service blueprint shall map:

1) customer actions and experience flow;

2) front-stage (visible to customer) service activities;

3) back-stage (not visible to customer) support activities;

4) support processes and systems.

b) The service blueprint shall identify all customer touchpoints — the points of interaction between the customer and the service provider.

### SE-03.4.2 Touchpoint Evaluation

a) For each identified touchpoint, the ranking entity shall evaluate:

1) the quality of the interaction at that touchpoint;

2) the customer's emotional response at that touchpoint;

3) the alignment of the touchpoint experience with the service provider's brand promise.

b) The ranking entity shall calculate a Touchpoint Quality Score for each touchpoint as:

$$TQS_j = \frac{1}{n_j} \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} s_{ij}$$

where $s_{ij}$ is the quality rating of touchpoint $j$ by customer $i$, and $n_j$ is the number of customers rating touchpoint $j$.

c) The Overall Touchpoint Quality Score shall be calculated as the weighted average of individual Touchpoint Quality Scores, with weights reflecting the relative importance of each touchpoint as determined by customer journey analysis.

### SE-03.4.3 Critical Incident Analysis

a) The ranking entity shall conduct critical incident analysis to identify service encounters that are particularly positive or negative in shaping the customer's overall service evaluation.

b) Critical incidents shall be categorised by:

1) the service dimension involved (tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy);

2) the touchpoint at which the incident occurred;

3) the severity and recoverability of the incident.

c) The ranking entity shall use critical incident data to identify service quality priorities and to contextualise aggregate quality scores.

## SE-03.5 Customer Satisfaction Index

### SE-03.5.1 Definition

The Customer Satisfaction Index (CSI) is a composite measure that synthesises customer satisfaction across the five service quality dimensions into a single index. The CSI shall be calculated for each ranked service provider.

### SE-03.5.2 Calculation Method

a) The CSI shall be calculated using the weighted gap model, extending the SERVQUAL Expectations-Perceptions framework:

$$CSI = \sum_{k=1}^{5} w_k \cdot \frac{P_k}{E_k}$$

where:

- $P_k$ = the mean perception score for dimension $k$ (Tangibles, Reliability, Responsiveness, Assurance, Empathy);

- $E_k$ = the mean expectation score for dimension $k$;

- $w_k$ = the weight for dimension $k$;

- $P_k / E_k$ is capped at 1.0 (perception cannot exceed expectation in the index ratio).

b) The default weights for the CSI shall be:

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Tangibles | 0.10 |
| Reliability | 0.30 |
| Responsiveness | 0.20 |
| Assurance | 0.25 |
| Empathy | 0.15 |

c) The default weights reflect the SERVQUAL literature consensus that Reliability is the most important dimension of service quality, followed by Assurance and Responsiveness.

### SE-03.5.3 Survey Requirements

a) The CSI survey shall measure both expectations and perceptions for each of the five dimensions.

b) The survey shall use paired questions (expectation item and corresponding perception item) for each Level 3 indicator.

c) The survey shall use a Likert-type scale with at least seven points for both expectation and perception items.

d) The ranking entity shall administer the CSI survey to a representative sample of recent service users (within the preceding 12 months) with a minimum sample size determined by the requirements specified in Chapter 7 of the core layer.

### SE-03.5.4 Interpretation

The CSI shall be interpreted as follows:

a) **CSI ≥ 0.90**: Excellent — the service consistently meets or exceeds customer expectations across all dimensions.

b) **0.75 ≤ CSI < 0.90**: Good — the service meets customer expectations in most dimensions with minor gaps.

c) **0.60 ≤ CSI < 0.75**: Adequate — the service meets basic expectations but has notable gaps in one or more dimensions.

d) **CSI < 0.60**: Poor — the service falls substantially below customer expectations and requires significant improvement.

## SE-03.6 Data Collection

### SE-03.6.1 Data Source Categories

The ranking entity shall collect data from the following categories of sources:

a) **Customer Surveys**: Structured surveys measuring service quality perceptions, expectations, and satisfaction. The CSI survey (SE-03.5) shall be the primary instrument.

b) **Mystery Shopping**: Structured assessments using trained evaluators who experience the service as regular customers. Mystery shopping shall be used to:

1) verify objective service delivery metrics (response time, accuracy, process compliance);

2) assess front-stage service quality that may not be captured by customer surveys;

3) provide comparable evaluations across service providers using standardised scenarios.

c) **Operational Data**: Internal operational metrics collected from service providers, including:

1) service volume and throughput data;

2) error rates and service failure data;

3) response time and resolution time data.

d) **Complaint and Review Data**: Customer complaint records and publicly available reviews, subject to the anti-fraud provisions of SE-01.5 as applicable.

e) **Accreditation and Certification Records**: Records of quality certifications, industry accreditations, and regulatory compliance.

### SE-03.6.2 Mystery Shopping Protocol

a) Mystery shopping shall be conducted by trained evaluators using standardised evaluation scripts. The evaluation script shall specify:

1) the service scenario to be enacted;

2) the specific service encounters to be evaluated;

3) the evaluation criteria and scoring rubric for each encounter;

4) the documentation requirements (notes, recordings, receipts).

b) The ranking entity shall ensure that mystery shopping evaluators:

1) are trained in the evaluation protocol and scoring criteria;

2) conduct evaluations independently without knowledge of other evaluators' results;

3) do not identify themselves as evaluators during the service encounter.

c) For each service provider, the ranking entity shall conduct a minimum of three independent mystery shopping evaluations to ensure reliability.

d) The ranking entity shall report inter-evaluator reliability statistics for mystery shopping data.

## SE-03.7 Scoring Methodology

### SE-03.7.1 Scoring Scale

a) Each Level 3 indicator shall be scored on a normalised scale from 0 to 100.

b) Survey-based indicators shall be scored using the CSI survey instrument's native scale, then normalised.

c) Mystery shopping indicators shall be scored using the standardised scoring rubric defined in the evaluation script.

### SE-03.7.2 Weighting

a) The default weight scheme for the five Level 1 dimensions shall be as follows:

| Dimension | Default Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Tangibles | 0.10 |
| Reliability | 0.30 |
| Responsiveness | 0.20 |
| Assurance | 0.25 |
| Empathy | 0.15 |

b) The default weights are aligned with the CSI weights specified in SE-03.5.2, reflecting the established priority order in service quality research.

c) For service sectors where physical environment is a primary value driver (e.g., luxury hospitality), the ranking entity may increase the weight of Tangibles to at most 0.20.

d) For service sectors where personal attention is critical (e.g., healthcare, counselling), the ranking entity may increase the weight of Empathy to at most 0.25.

### SE-03.7.3 Aggregation

a) Scores shall be aggregated using the weighted sum method specified in Chapter 8 of the core layer.

b) The overall service quality score and the CSI shall both be reported in the ranking publication.

c) The ranking entity shall report the Top-3 and Bottom-3 touchpoint quality scores for each ranked service provider.

## SE-03.8 Alignment with International Standards

### SE-03.8.1 SERVQUAL Framework Alignment

a) The indicator system shall be consistent with the five-dimensional SERVQUAL model as originally proposed by Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Berry (1988), with extensions for digital service contexts.

b) The ranking entity shall document any extensions or modifications to the SERVQUAL model and shall justify their inclusion based on the specific service context being ranked.

### SE-03.8.2 ISO 56000 Alignment

a) For service providers that employ innovation as a service quality differentiator, the ranking entity may include innovation-related indicators consistent with ISO 56000:2020, such as:

1) the rate of service innovation introduction;

2) the adoption rate of new service delivery technologies;

3) customer perception of service innovation value.

---

## SE-03.9 Publication and Transparency

### SE-03.9.1 Publication Requirements

In addition to the requirements specified in Chapter 9 of the core layer, service quality ranking publications shall include:

a) the CSI for each ranked service provider, including dimension-level perception and expectation scores;

b) the service blueprint reference used for touchpoint identification;

c) the mystery shopping methodology summary, including the number of evaluations per provider;

d) the survey methodology summary, including sample size, method, and response rate;

e) sector-specific weight adjustments, if any.

### SE-03.9.2 Service Provider Identification

a) Each ranked service provider shall be uniquely identified using:

1) the legal entity name and registration details;

2) the specific service scope included in the evaluation;

3) the geographic scope of the evaluation.

---

> **Note**: This extension module is part of ICO Std 2002 (Tianji Ranking Methodology Standard). Service quality evaluation is fundamentally about the customer's experience, which is subjective, context-dependent, and shaped by expectations. The SERVQUAL-based approach used in this module captures the gap between expectation and perception, but ranking entities should recognise that expectations vary across cultures, demographics, and service contexts. The CSI and touchpoint analysis are complementary tools: the CSI provides a summary measure, while touchpoint analysis reveals where in the service journey quality is created or destroyed. Ranking entities should use both to produce rankings that are meaningful and actionable for both service providers and their customers.
